Think of your computer as a body

Seven parts, seven jobs. Pick a point on the diagram to see what it does and why it's there.

Brain

CPU

The part that thinks. Every calculation and decision your computer makes passes through here first, one instruction at a time, thousands of times a second.

Faster cores mean less waiting — especially for anything you can't run on a video card.

Eyes

GPU

What renders the world you actually see — every frame, texture, and color on screen. If the CPU decides what should happen, the GPU is what shows you it happening.

The part to spend on if you game, edit video, or run anything visual.

Short-term memory

RAM

Holds whatever you're doing right now — open tabs, open programs, the document you haven't saved. Close the lid, and it's gone. That's the trade: it's fast because it doesn't have to remember forever.

More of it means more things open at once without slowing down.

Long-term memory

Storage

Everything you keep — photos, files, the game you haven't finished. Unlike RAM, it holds on after the power's off. It's the filing cabinet, not the desk.

Solid-state storage reads faster; it's why your computer boots in seconds, not minutes.

Nervous system

Motherboard

Connects every other part to every other part. No signal, no power, and no data moves through this machine without passing across it first.

Unglamorous, but it decides what you're allowed to upgrade later.

Heart

Power supply

Pumps power to every other part, converted down from the wall to exactly what each component can safely take. Nothing above runs without it.

Worth not skimping on — it's the one part that can take others down with it.

Lungs

Cooling

Moves heat out before it builds up. Every part above generates warmth just by working — cooling is what lets them keep working under pressure instead of slowing down to protect themselves.

Quieter, better cooling usually means a machine that stays fast under load.
