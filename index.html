Think of your computer like a human body.

No computer expertise needed, here is a simple breakdown of how each part operates and integrates like the human body.

(Click any card below for a popup reference image).

Central Processing Unit (CPU)

The Brain

The CPU acts as the brain of a computer, every decision runs through here, it is responsible for interpreting and executing "instructions" from the software and hardware. The CPU processes almost every task you do on your computer.

Graphics Card

The Eyes

The graphics card acts as the eyes of a computer, every pixel you see on your computer is rendered and displayed on your computer screen by taking data from the CPU. The GPU is housed in the graphics card and acts as the main engine to process visual data.

Power Supply (PSU)

The Heart

The PSU acts as the heart of a computer, it takes raw electricity from outlets and converts it into usable energy for you PC. The PSU is a core part of a computer.

Motherboard

The Spine

The motherboard acts as the spine of a computer, it houses important parts like CPU and RAM into place, it takes electricity from the power supply and routes it to different places, and provides data pathways so different parts can communicate with eacher other.

Random Access Memory (RAM)

Short-Term Memory

RAM acts as the short-term memory of a computer, it holds the data that the processer needs at the moment to run apps and open files for multi-tasking and gaming/heavy tasks.

Solid-State Drive (SSD)

Long-Term Memory

The SSD acts as the long-term memory of a computer, it holds data permanently; saving your operating system, games, apps and files even when your computer is off.

Case & Cooling

Skin & Lungs

The case acts like the skin of a computer, it holds and protects your hardware from damage. The cooling system acts like the lungs of a computer, the fans prevent the PC from overheating by pushing hot air out of the case and pulling cold air in.

Tested with real everyday buyers.

I sat down with my parents, walked through the build guides using the body analogy, and answered their honest questions.

Questions They Asked Me

Question 1

"Why does the $500 computer have a graphics card if it's not meant for gaming?"

My Answer: It helps with smooth video playback and takes the load off the basic CPU, but the card itself is too weak to render 3D game environments quickly.

Question 2

"Is the $1000 one good enough for massive Excel spreadsheets and running games like Minecraft?"

My Answer: Yes, the 6-core processor handles heavy multitasking perfectly, and the entry-level graphics card can run games like Minecraft very smoothly.

Question 3

"Why is the last one so incredibly expensive compared to the others?"

My Answer: The RX 9070 XT graphics card alone is a massive investment meant for running top-tier games in 4K resolution. You are paying a premium for luxury graphics and huge, ultra-fast storage.

The Result & Choice

My parents were most interested in the balance between price and lifespan. They appreciated the "brain and short-term memory" analogy to understand why the $500 build might slow down if too many tabs were open.

Chose: Balanced Build — $999

They ultimately chose the $1000 tier because they felt the $500 build would need to be replaced too soon (only a 1-2 year runway), and the $2,441 build was overkill for a general family and homework PC. The $1000 build hit the sweet spot for their needs.
